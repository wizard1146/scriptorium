In Utopia, players choose a race that determines their province's inherent strengths, weaknesses, unique abilities, and available spells, such as fast-attacking Avians, magic-focused Elves, or economically strong Dwarves. They also select a personality, which layers on specialized bonuses, starting advantages, and tactical perks, from aggressive Warrior boosts to powerful mystic benefits. Together, race and personality shape a player’s playstyle, strategic options, and role within their kingdom.

Looking for the numbers? This page is the short guide to what each race and personality is about. The exact bonuses, penalties, units, abilities and spells for this Age are on Current Changes: Age {{age}}.

Races

The races in play this Age. Each summary links to that race's full entry, with its history.

Avian

A mix of bird and man, Avians have the unique ability to fly. Their natural wilderness lifestyle and speed make them efficient and powerful warriors, and they breed at a ferocious rate. In play they are a fast, aggressive race: armies travel and train quickly and are cheap to keep, which rewards players who like to attack often. The trade-offs are no access to stables or war horses, and pricier magic.

Full entry and history: Avian

Dark Elf

The subterranean counterpart of the Elves, Dark Elves are known for their magical ability and for being evil individuals who enjoy suffering and pain. Their hostile underground home taught them special offensive maneuvers, and their offensive spells hit harder and cost less than average. They make strong offensive mages and can even train thieves from specialist credits, but their provinces are fragile against enemy sabotage and grow more slowly.

Full entry and history: Dark Elf

Dryad

A magical race of treefolk, the Dryads are caretakers of nature with a unique connection to the land and to the creatures of their woodlands. They are peaceable and reluctant to fight, but very strong when they decide to. In play they are a defensive race: they lose fewer troops when defending, grow quickly, and can swell their population for a few days with their unique ability. Attacking is slower, offensive spells cost more, and they cannot build forts.

Full entry and history: Dryad

Dwarf

Friendly but powerful, Dwarves are by far the most technologically advanced race in Utopia, with huge skill in engineering, mining and metallurgy. Over time they have become excellent at building and using their land: construction is fast and cheap, and their buildings work more efficiently. Razing cuts both ways for them, since they take less damage from it and deal more. They cannot accelerate construction and have a huge appetite for food.

Full entry and history: Dwarf

Elf

An intelligent people known for their magical abilities, Elves give high priority to research of the arcane and master mystical energies more easily than other races. They are naturally oriented towards magic but versatile enough to attack as well, which makes them good mages or supporting attacker/mages. Their spells are stronger and last longer, at the cost of more expensive drafting.

Full entry and history: Elf

Faery

A race of magical creatures unknown to many, Faeries excel at magic and subterfuge, largely thanks to their small size, yet are often called the most peaceful of the Utopian races. They keep their own spells running longer, recover mana faster, and enemy spells sometimes simply fail against them. The price of being fragile but noble is a smaller population and heavier casualties in battle.

Full entry and history: Faery

Halfling

Tiny humans famous for their quickness and thievery, Halflings are mediocre warriors, but their small stature brings many unique advantages, above all a natural ability to get far more out of thievery operations. They fit more people into the same land, recover stealth quickly, and lose fewer thieves on sabotage, though they draft more slowly.

Full entry and history: Halfling

Human

The most scholarly and noble of the races, Humans have always taken the greatest interest in learning about their world and building better tools to make life easier. They are often seen as greedy and selfish, but they know how to make money. Income is high, troops are cheap to train and quick to draft, and mercenaries cost less, although military wages weigh heavier. Overall, Humans are versatile.

Full entry and history: Human

Orc

The most evil of the races, Orcs are cruel, selfish and arrogant lovers of destruction and chaos. They are among the most ferocious and efficient warriors on the planet, and their ranks hold one of the most powerful units in the game, the dreadful Ogre. Orcs tend to be focused attackers: they gain more from attacks, inflict heavier casualties and draft cheaply, but suffer more from enemy sabotage and sorcery. A good pick for players who do not want to bother with heavy magic or thievery.

Full entry and history: Orc

Undead

An unnatural species with unique abilities, the Undead are generally rotting to pieces and carry the plague, a highly contagious disease that kills the living but seems to give them the strength they need to survive, with them wherever they walk. They lose far fewer troops, are immune to plague and need no food, but they cannot build hospitals.

Full entry and history: Undead

Personalities

The personalities in play this Age. Each summary links to that personality's full entry, with its history.

Artisan

A skilled manual worker who crafts items that may be functional or strictly decorative, the Artisan gets more out of the buildings in their province. Artisan provinces build bigger and produce more, construction is cheaper, and they shrug off Greed, Incite Riots and Fool’s Gold. When they raze an enemy’s buildings, they recover part of them as building credits.

Full entry and history: Artisan

Cleric

Clerics assist their armies on the battlefield personally, healing wounded soldiers to reduce casualties, and support their comrades with magical blessings. It is a defensive, supportive personality: stronger defensive units, less damage from instant spells, and a chance for self-spells to last twice as long. Its spellbook is built around protection and insight.

Full entry and history: Cleric

General

The General’s focus is on the strategic side of war, and leadership is its own reward. Generals field an extra general, train elites quickly and cheaply, and can even train an elite for every two specialist credits. Attacks hit the enemy harder when several generals march together.

Full entry and history: General

Heretic

The Heretic goes against the fundamental norms and does not care, perfectly happy dissenting from accepted arcane beliefs and doctrine. In play it is a hybrid of thief and mage: strong offensive thievery and magic, fewer thieves lost, immunity to Expose Thieves and more effective guilds, with a burst of mana and stealth after each successful attack.

Full entry and history: Heretic

Mystic

A master of magic, the Mystic has experience getting the most out of the world of sorcery. Being led by one of their own gives a province extraordinary ability in the mystic arts, including access to powerful spells that others never get. Guilds are far more effective, mana recovers faster, and spells grow stronger while your mana stays high.

Full entry and history: Mystic

Necromancer

A dark caster who builds on wizard power and cheaper runes, and who feeds on the battlefield: after each successful attack, part of the enemy’s killed units rise again as wizards, soldiers and peasants for the Necromancer’s own province. Its spellbook leans on death and fear.

Numbers for the Necromancer: Current Changes

Rogue

Widely considered evil to the core, the Rogue is an enigmatic persona with extensive experience in thievery, fraud and crime, which in the Utopian world can be used for both good and evil. Thieves’ dens work far better, stealth returns faster, every thievery operation is unlocked, and Rogue provinces can keep operating even while overpopulated.

Full entry and history: Rogue

Sage

The Sage has the wisdom of the ages and infinite knowledge, and can provide unique guidance in a world made up of many different areas. Sage provinces are more efficient in the domains of arts and sciences: books and scientists are generated faster, they start with extra scientists, and their science effects build up through a war.

Full entry and history: Sage

Tactician

The Tactician’s focus is on the strategic side of war, allowing swift and accurate movement of troops. Leadership is its own reward, and the Tactician’s cunning turns strategy into an edge: attacks arrive sooner, specialist credits and drafting come faster, intel costs no thieves, and successful attacks in war destroy some of the enemy’s resources.

Full entry and history: Tactician

Warrior

Aggressive, powerful and forceful are all adjectives that come to mind when describing the Warrior. Leadership through military power is what being a Warrior is all about: better offensive efficiency, stronger mercenaries and prisoners that can be sent in larger numbers, and attacks that destroy part of the enemy’s population.

Full entry and history: Warrior

War Hero

Great military leaders, War Heroes are among the finest commanders in Utopia. A background in war and a legacy of heroism creates an aura of legitimacy around any leader. They gain more from war, lose less honor and get more out of honor’s effects, and their massacres kill extra peasants, thieves and wizards.

Full entry and history: War Hero
